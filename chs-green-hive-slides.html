Read this first

⚠ Safety rules: we mix water + electricity

Follow these every single time. A teacher or adult is always present when you build.

🔌 220 V = adults only

Only an adult or the school electrician plugs in, unplugs, or touches anything connected to the wall outlet. Students work only on the 12 V and 5 V side.

⛔ Unplug before touching

Always unplug the power before you change any wire. Never work on a circuit that's on.

💧 Water never touches 220 V

Adapters and outlets stay indoors or in a closed box, away from mist and pipes. Use an outlet with an RCD (shock protection).

🥽 Eyes & hands

Safety glasses when cutting pipe, drilling, or testing the pump (100 psi can spray hard). Gloves for PVC cement.

🪜 Working high

Nozzles go 2.5–3 m up. Use a proper ladder with someone holding it. No standing on chairs.

🔥 Hot tools

A soldering iron or heat gun burns at 300°C+. Put it back in its stand, and unplug it when you're done.

Golden rule: if something smells burning, sparks, or gets hot → unplug it and call the teacher. Don't try to fix it while it's on.

CHS GreenHive build guide3
What we mean by "misting"

💦 Water only, through very tiny holes: a mini shower that doesn't drip

  • A tube runs along the walkway roof, with a nozzle every 40–60 cm (about 10 per walkway)
  • Each nozzle has a very tiny hole: 0.3 mm, thinner than a pencil line
  • The pump pushes the water hard (about 100 psi; a faucet is about 30), so it bursts out as very fine drops, like a mini shower or a spray bottle
  • The drops are so small they evaporate in the air, and evaporation pulls heat out of the air. That's the cooling
  • Just water. No chemicals, no electricity at the nozzles
Big drops, or a wet floor? The pressure is too low or a nozzle is worn. Clean the filter, check for leaks, or replace the nozzle.

How the brain thinks

The rules, in plain words

💨 Misting

Mist ONLY IF all of these are true:

  • ✅ It's a school day, 7:00 AM – 5:00 PM
  • ✅ The heat index is 33°C or more (PAGASA "Extreme Caution")
  • ✅ It is NOT raining
  • ✅ The tank has water
  • ✅ The air isn't already super humid (under 80%)

Then it sprays in pulses: 1 min ON, 2 min OFF. At 42°C or more ("Danger"): 1.5 min ON, 1 min OFF.
Pulses = the floor stays dry and we use less water.

🌱 Garden watering

Water the garden ONLY IF:

  • ✅ The soil is dry (under 35%)
  • ✅ It's a cool time: 5–8 AM or 4–6:30 PM (less water evaporates)
  • ✅ It is NOT raining, and the tank has water

It waters for up to 20 min, waits 45 min for the water to soak in, and repeats until the soil reaches 60%.
If the soil is really dry (under 20%), it waters right away.

When in doubt → water OFF. A broken sensor, an empty tank, or a lost connection all turn everything OFF. That's called fail-safe.
CHS GreenHive build guide4
Meet the parts · the brain

Orange Pi One: our tiny computer

  • A full Linux computer the size of a credit card
  • Runs our program chs-green-hive.py all day, every day
  • The 40 metal pins (the "header") connect to sensors and relays
  • Plugs into the school network with an Ethernet cable
3 things that can wreck it:
1. Power through the wrong plug: it uses a round 4.0 × 1.7 mm barrel, 5 V. Not USB.
2. Putting 5 V or 12 V on a pin: the pins only handle 3.3 V.
3. Overheating: stick on the heatsink and keep the box in the shade.

It's missing a few things, so we add them:

It has no…So we add…
Wi-Fian Ethernet cable to the school router
a way to read soil sensorsADS1115 board (reads voltages)
a clock batteryDS3231 clock board (remembers the time)
CHS GreenHive build guide5
Meet the parts · the eyes

Sensors: what each one tells the brain

🌡 SHT31: temp + humidity

Measures the air. The program turns it into the heat index ("feels like" temperature). Put it in the shade!

🌱 Soil sensor ×2 (capacitive)

Push it into the soil up to the line. Wet soil gives a lower voltage. Get the black flat type, not the metal fork (it rusts).

🔢 ADS1115

A translator: turns the soil sensor's voltage into a number the Orange Pi understands.

🌧 Rain sensor

When drops land on the plate, the little board says "rain!". Mount it facing the sky.

🪣 Float switch

A floating ring inside the tank. When the water gets too low, it tells the brain to stop the pump.

🕑 DS3231 clock

A coin battery keeps the time, so "7 AM" is still correct after a power cut.

CHS GreenHive build guide6
Meet the parts · the switches

Relay board: a tiny signal flips a big switch

The Orange Pi's pins are too weak to run a pump. A relay is like a light switch that the computer can "press".

8-channel relay board

8 switches: 1 pump + 4 valves + 3 spare. You'll hear a "click" when one turns on, and a small LED lights up.

3 screw holes on each relay: COM = power in · NO ("normally open") = power out to the valve ✅ use this · NC = leave empty.
CHS GreenHive build guide7
Meet the parts · the water side

Pump, valves, nozzles & drippers

⚙ 12 V pump

Pushes water at about 100 psi (much stronger than a faucet). Strong pressure = fine mist.

🚰 Valve ×4

An electric tap. 12 V = open, no power = closed ("normally-closed").

💨 Mist nozzle

A super tiny hole (0.3 mm). Pressurised water bursts out as a very fine spray, like a mini shower that doesn't drip. About 10 per walkway.

💧 Drip hose

A black hose with drippers at each plant. Water goes straight to the roots.

🧽 Filter

Catches sand and dirt before it can clog the tiny nozzles.

Buying tip for the 2 garden valves: cheap valves need pressure to open. Gravity from the tank isn't enough, so buy valves labelled "zero pressure" or "direct acting" for the garden. The 2 mist valves are after the pump, so the normal cheap ones work there.

1Set up the Orange Pi

a
On a laptop, download Armbian for Orange Pi One from armbian.com/orange-pi-one (pick the Debian Bookworm one).
b
Write it to the microSD card with balenaEtcher. Put the card in the Orange Pi.
c
Stick the heatsink on the big chip. Connect the Ethernet cable, then the 5 V barrel plug. The red LED turns on.
d
Find its IP address on the router page (ask IT), then connect from the laptop: ssh root@<ip> (first password: 1234, then make your own).
e
Copy our project folder to the Pi, into /opt/greenhive (use WinSCP: drag and drop).

Then type these commands on the Pi:

# set the Philippine time zone
sudo timedatectl set-timezone Asia/Manila

# turn on the sensor bus (I2C)
sudo armbian-config
#  → System → Hardware → tick "i2c0" → Save → Reboot

# install the checker tool + Python
sudo apt update
sudo apt install -y i2c-tools python3
✅ Done when: ls /dev/i2c-* shows /dev/i2c-0.

Build · step 2 of 8 · Team Sparks ⚡ · power OFF while wiring

2Wire the sensors

The 3 small boards (SHT31, ADS1115, DS3231) share the same 4 wires. This is called the I2C bus. Connect each board to the same pins:

Board pin→ Orange Pi pinWire color
VIN / VCC / VDDpin 1 (3.3 V)red
GNDpin 6 (ground)black
SDApin 3green
SCLpin 5white

Soil sensors: red → 3.3 V · black → GND · yellow (signal) → ADS1115 A0 (Garden A) and A1 (Garden B).

Rain sensor board: VCC → 3.3 V · GND → GND · DO → pin 33.

Float switch: one wire → GND, the other → pin 31, plus a 10 kΩ resistor from pin 31 to 3.3 V.

How to find pin 1 on the header

Pin 1 has a square pad or a small "1" or triangle printed next to it. Odd numbers (1, 3, 5…) are one row, even numbers (2, 4, 6…) the other. Count carefully: one pin off can kill a sensor.

Check the boards answer:

sudo i2cdetect -y 0
# you should see: 44 (SHT31) 48 (ADS1115) 68 (DS3231)
Use female-female jumper wires on the pins. A tiny dab of hot glue keeps them from wiggling loose.

Important: the relay coils need 5 V, but the Pi's pins are 3.3 V. So pull off the little "JD-VCC" jumper and feed JD-VCC + its GND from the 12 V → 5 V converter (set it to 5.0 V with a multimeter first!).

If the relays don't click reliably, buy a relay board marked "3.3 V trigger".

4Table test: make sure the brain works

# start the program for real (not simulation)
cd /opt/greenhive
sudo python3 chs-green-hive.py --live

On a phone or laptop on the same network, open http://<pi-ip>:8080. The top pill should say ● LIVE.

Test each one ✔

  • Press Run 5 min on each zone → its relay clicks and its LED lights up (mist zones also click R1 = pump)
  • Breathe on the SHT31 → the humidity number goes up
  • Unplug the float switch → the dashboard says tank low, locked and everything turns OFF
  • Drip water on the rain plate → "Rain detected", everything OFF
  • Press EMERGENCY STOP → everything turns OFF at once

🌱 Calibrate the soil sensors

Every sensor is a bit different, so teach the program what "dry" and "wet" mean:

  1. Hold the sensor in the air → note the raw number = dry
  2. Put it in a glass of water (up to the line) → note it = wet
  3. Write both in chs-green-hive-config.json: soil_dry_raw and soil_wet_raw
✅ Done when every checkbox works. Only then do we add 12 V and water. Fixing problems on a table is 10× easier than on a roof!

Build · step 5 of 8 · Team Sparks ⚡ · with the teacher

5Connect the pump & valves (12 V side)

For each valve / pump

Relay COM ← red +12 V · relay NO → the valve's red wire · the valve's black wire → black −12 V.

The diode (₱5 lifesaver)

Put a 1N4007 diode across each valve's 2 wires, silver stripe on the + (red) side. It eats the zap the coil makes when it switches off.

Test it (still no water)

Plug in the 12 V supply (adult). Press Run 5 min → the valve goes "clunk" and the pump hums for a second. ✅

Which wire do I use?

🔌 The right wire for each job

Thin wires are fine for signals. Wires that carry power over a distance must be thicker, or the valve won't get enough voltage to open.

Box → float switch, rain sensor2- or 3-core cable0.5 mm²20 many

Joining wires inside the box: lever connectors (Wago 221) or screw terminals. Put a ferrule (metal tube) on stranded wire ends.
Joining wires outdoors: only heat-shrink with glue or gel-filled connectors. Never just electrical tape: rain gets in.
Label everything: put a sticker with "R2 walkway" on both ends of each cable. Future you will say thanks.

Build · step 7 of 8 · everyone · adult on the ladder

7Mount everything outdoors

📦 The control box

  • Electronics go inside the IP65 box, in the shade, under a roof edge
  • Every cable enters from the bottom through a cable gland (squeeze nut)
  • Make a drip loop: the cable dips down before going up into the box, so rain drips off
  • Lock it, or mount it high, so nobody plays with it

💨 The mist line

  • Nozzles 2.5–3 m high, along the side of the walkway, not over the middle
  • Point them slightly down and across
  • Tie the tube with clips or cable ties every 50 cm
  • Goal: cool air, dry floor. If the floor gets wet, raise the nozzles or remove some.

🌡 The sensors

  • Temperature sensor: inside a white vented box, 1.5 m high, never in direct sun
  • Soil sensors: push in up to the line, near the plant roots
  • Rain sensor: tilted slightly, facing the sky, not under a roof
  • Cables run in flexible conduit along the wall

Build · step 8 of 8 · everyone

8First real run 🎉

Final checklist

  • Tank full, ball valve open, filter clean
  • Run each zone with Run 5 min: walkway mist, gathering mist, Garden A, Garden B
  • Fine mist, no big drops, floor stays dry after 5 min
  • Drippers drip at every plant
  • Close the ball valve → the tank level drops → it locks (or lift the float)
  • Set a control PIN in the config so only teachers can press buttons
  • Change the node_token to your own secret word

Make it start by itself every morning

# create the auto-start file
sudo nano /etc/systemd/system/greenhive.service
[Unit]
Description=CHS GreenHive
After=network-online.target
[Service]
WorkingDirectory=/opt/greenhive
ExecStart=/usr/bin/python3 /opt/greenhive/chs-green-hive.py --live
Restart=always
[Install]
WantedBy=multi-user.target
sudo systemctl enable --now greenhive   # start now + on every boot

Keep it running

🔧 Maintenance & "it's not working!"

📅 Maintenance schedule

Every week

  • Check the dashboard log for warnings
  • Flush the mist lines (Run 5 min each)
  • Look for leaks and wet floors

Every month

  • Clean the filter screen
  • Soak the nozzles in vinegar for 1 hour
  • Clean the tank; keep it covered
  • Wipe the rain sensor plate

Every year

  • Replace the rain sensor; check the cables for cracks
ProblemTry this
Relay clicks but the valve doesn't openCheck the 12 V with the multimeter at the valve; check the fuse; is the valve's arrow pointing the right way? Garden valve: is it a "zero pressure" type?
Nothing clicks at allIs the JD-VCC converter giving 5 V? Are the jumper wires on the right pins?
Mist is weak or dripsClogged nozzle (vinegar soak) or a dirty filter; check the tube joints for leaks

"Tank low" but the tank is fullThe float switch is upside down or its wire is broken (beep test)
Wrong time / mists at nightCheck the DS3231 battery and the time zone (timedatectl)

Words you'll hear

📖 Mini dictionary

Pin / GPIO

A metal pin on the Orange Pi that the program can turn on/off or read.

Relay

A switch that a computer can flip. It clicks!

I2C

4 shared wires that several sensors use to talk to the Pi.

Heat index

How hot it feels: temperature + humidity together.

Volt (V)

Electric "pressure". The Pi uses 3.3 V and 5 V, the pump 12 V, the wall 220 V.

Amp (A)

How much electricity flows. Thick wire = more amps.

psi

Water pressure. Faucet ≈ 30, our pump ≈ 100.

mm² / AWG

Wire thickness. Bigger mm² = thicker wire.

Fuse

A tiny wire that melts on purpose to stop a short circuit.

Diode

A one-way street for electricity. It protects against zaps.

NC valve

Normally-closed: stays shut when there's no power.

Fail-safe

If something breaks, the system goes to the safe state (water OFF).
