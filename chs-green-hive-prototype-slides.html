First: what we mean by "misting"

💦 Water only, through very tiny holes: a mini shower that doesn't drip

  • A tube carries water, with several nozzle outlets along it (one every 40–60 cm)
  • Each nozzle has a very tiny hole (0.2–0.4 mm, thinner than a pencil line)
  • A pump pushes the water hard (70–100 psi), so it bursts out as very fine drops, like a mini shower or spray bottle
  • The drops are so small they evaporate in the air, and evaporation takes heat away. That's the cooling!
  • No chemicals, no electricity near the water outlets: just water
This is exactly how the big school system works. The prototype is the same thing, just shorter: 1 m of tube and 3 nozzles instead of 10 m and 10 nozzles.

The water parts, explained

What each mini misting part does

⚙ 12 V mini diaphragm pump

Sucks water from the bucket and pushes it out at 70–115 psi. Has a built-in pressure switch. Look for "0.5–0.8 MPa".

🚰 ¼″ mini solenoid valve

An electric tap: 12 V = open. "Normally closed", so it shuts when the power is off. The arrow on it = the water direction.

💦 Mist nozzles (×3)

Brass nozzles with a 0.3 mm hole. They screw into slip-lock tees on the ¼″ tube. Water in → fine spray out.

〰 ¼″ (6 mm) misting tube

Strong plastic tube for high pressure. Cut it straight, push it into the fittings until it clicks. One end plug at the far end.

🧽 Inline filter

Between the bucket and the pump. A grain of sand can block a 0.3 mm hole!

💧 USB mini pump (plant)

Sits inside the bucket and gently pushes water up an aquarium tube to the plant pot. Low pressure = perfect for watering.

🟡 Mini float switch

Inside the bucket. When the water gets low it says "stop!", so the pumps never run dry.

🔋 12 V → 5 V converter

Makes 5 V from the 12 V adapter, for the relay coils and the USB plant pump. Set it to 5.0 V with the multimeter first.

Wiring · everything unplugged while you connect

🔌 How to wire the mini prototype

Diode rule: a 1N4007 across the pump, the valve and the USB pump (silver stripe on the + side). Never connect 12 V or 5 V + to an Orange Pi pin. Keep the 12 V adapter's plug far from the water.

GreenHive Mini9
Build it

🛠 7 steps, about 1–2 weekends

1
Set up the Orange Pi. Armbian on the microSD, network cable, enable I2C, copy the project to /opt/greenhive (real build guide, step 1).
2
Sensors on the breadboard. SHT31 + ADS1115 on 3.3 V / GND / SDA / SCL. Check: sudo i2cdetect -y 0 shows 44 and 48.
3
Relay board. IN1 = pin 7, IN2 = pin 11, IN4 = pin 15. Take off the JD-VCC jumper, feed it 5 V from the converter.
4
Table test, no water. Run the program, press Run 5 min: the relays click, the valve "clunks", the pump hums.
5
Mist line. Bucket → filter → pump → valve → ¼″ tube with 3 nozzle tees and an end plug. Leak test with the tube lying in the tray first.
6
Plant + safety sensors. USB pump and float switch inside the bucket, aquarium tube to the pot, soil sensor in the soil up to the line.
7
Build the mini campus. Walkway inside a plastic tray, popsicle/wood "roof" holding the mist tube, SHT31 about 30 cm from the nozzles, rain sensor on top, the tray draining back into the bucket.
Test after every step. Fix the problem before you move to the next one.

cd /opt/greenhive

     --config chs-green-hive-config.prototype.json

Open http://<pi-ip>:8080 on a phone or laptop. You'll see 2 zones: Mini walkway misting and Plant pot watering.

Calibrate the soil sensor first

Sensor in the air = dry number; in a glass of water = wet number. Put both in the prototype config (soil_dry_raw, soil_wet_raw).

Safety, mini edition

⚠ Small, but still water + electricity + pressure

Electronics up, water down

Orange Pi, relays and adapters sit higher than the water and far from the spray. The walkway is inside a tray.

Unplug before changing wires

All adapters out of the wall before you move any wire. An adult plugs them in.

Pressure = eye safety

The pump pushes up to 100 psi. Safety glasses when testing, never point a nozzle at a face, never open a fitting while the pump runs.

Clean water only

Use clean tap water with the filter in place. Empty and dry the bucket after demos so nothing grows in it.

Check for heat

After 10 minutes, touch the adapter, relay and pump. Warm is OK; too hot to hold = unplug and check.

Never 220 V in the model

Only plug-in adapters (12 V and 5 V). No cutting wall cables, ever.
